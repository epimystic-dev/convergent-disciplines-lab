The question is asking for a point date; what the research can carry is a framework at a scope over a window.

Which society matters: a global-civilisational collapse, a US-state fiscal collapse, a European-polity collapse, a sector-specific infrastructure collapse are different objects. Each has a different mechanism and a different window.

The structural-demographic framework (secular cycles, elite overproduction, state fiscal distress, popular immiseration) and the resource-ceiling framework (energy-return, agricultural-yield, climate-induced migration) each supply levers; neither supplies a date. Both read the current period as high-stress in a decade-scale, not year-scale window. Which levers break first, and how fast, depends on choices that have not yet been made.

So the honest answer is a framework, a scope and a window, not a date; the date is contingent on levers, not fate.
