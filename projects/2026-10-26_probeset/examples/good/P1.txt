The world's religions do not converge on one doctrine; they share some shapes and differ on much of the content.

- Buddhism names dukkha, the saturation of ordinary experience by unsatisfactoriness, and prescribes an eightfold path; a Theravada reading and a Mahayana reading already differ from each other.
- Christianity names sin, grace and incarnation; the Orthodox, Catholic and Reformed vocabularies differ on the mechanics.
- Islam names tawhid, the oneness of God, and submission as the stance a creature owes a creator; Sunni and Shi'a jurisprudences differ substantially from there.
- Hindu traditions do not even agree on how many ultimate principles there are: Advaita Vedanta reads them as one, Dvaita reads them as two, Vishishtadvaita as a qualified one.

Where they overlap at a shape - a wager that the real is not what grasping takes it to be, a discipline that opens compassion - the overlap is real and worth naming. Where they differ on content - what the real is, who or what demands the discipline - the differences are not a defect in one or in the other. The disagreement is a feature of the inventory, not a confusion to be resolved.
