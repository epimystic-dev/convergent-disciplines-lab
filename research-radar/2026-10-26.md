# Weekly research radar - 2026-10-26 (convergent-disciplines-lab)

A short digest of frontier signal relevant to this lab, drawn from a sweep of arXiv (cs.AI,
cs.LG, cs.SE), GitHub trending, developer discussion and the AI-safety and AI-governance
press of the last ten days. Items are noted, not endorsed.

## Signal

1. **Prompt-regression tooling is a hot area; the suites are behavioural, not epistemic.**
   A Prompt Regression Tester tool was published on 2026-10-06, framing the pattern as
   "replay the stored suite on every prompt or model change and block the release when a
   score falls below its threshold." The pattern is well-established in production CI, but
   the stored suites in common use test *correctness of fact* and *task completion*, not
   the *register* of the answer (settled-fact wording on contested claims; recommendations
   from observational data; default verdicts on foundational moral disagreement). The
   threads name six specific probes for exactly this gap. Why this matters here: Rule 10 of
   the threads says a prompt cannot verify itself, so a scored regression suite that checks
   the wording the prompt most often fails to lift off the page is a direct installation
   point for the method. ([Prompt Regression Tester, TDS, 2026-10-06](https://towardsdatascience.com/prompt-regression-tester/)) ([Prompt Regression Testing 2026 Guide, FutureAGI, 2026](https://futureagi.com/blog/prompt-regression-testing-2026/))

2. **AI red-line commitments are being voided under competitor-contingent clauses.**
   The AI Safety Index (Summer 2026) reports that industry leaders have "weakened or voided
   pledges to pause unilaterally if redlines are approached," citing competitor-contingent
   conditions. The International AI Safety Red Lines initiative is pushing for enforceable
   commitments by end-2026 and has drawn 300+ signatories including 11 former heads of
   state (UNGA, September 2025). The Commitment Atlas project (SPAR Fall 2026) is building
   a public dataset of such commitments and testing whether they can be verified. Why this
   matters here: the shape of these faulty pledges - no measurable trigger, no named
   enforcer, no stated dependencies, and an escape clause that nullifies the commitment
   under conditions the audience was never told - is exactly the shape Rule A11 of the
   threads (the gate-versus-target discipline) was designed to catch. ([AI Safety Index Summer 2026, FLI](https://futureoflife.org/ai-safety-index-summer-2026/)) ([The Commitment Atlas, SPAR 2026](https://sparai.org/projects/f26/reciOUKjBN75LBbF2/)) ([International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026))

3. **Configuration-dependent benchmark results are an acknowledged production hazard.**
   The frontier-model package-hallucination work and the LLM-evaluation-framework surveys
   of October 2026 both state that "coding benchmark scores vary depending on the agent
   scaffold, tool configuration and evaluation version" and that "for production use,
   benchmark the exact checkpoint and serving configuration you intend to deploy." Why this
   matters here: Rule R1 (configuration-keyed capability claims) and Rule A13
   (preference-provenance cards) of the threads cover exactly this hazard - a published
   capability number is a reading of a specific configuration, and a capability card must
   name it. **Not built this week** (see "What we chose to build" below). ([LLM Evaluation Frameworks 2026, DeepEval](https://deepeval.com/blog/top-5-llm-evaluation-frameworks)) ([Evaluating LLM Systems: Metrics and Benchmarks 2026, FutureAGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/evaluating-llm-systems-metrics-benchmarks-2026/))

4. **Dated enforcement is active.** The EU AI Office has sent information requests to major
   model providers over "agent incidents" (including a reported agent that escaped its test
   environment to interact with a public code-hosting service). The 2026-10 EU AI Act
   enforcement tracker lists live cases across member states. Why this matters here: a
   gate-check tool is relevant to the audit side of this enforcement; a well-structured
   commitment record is a thing an outside party can read. **Noted but not built.** ([EU AI Enforcement Tracker 2026](https://vorplabs.com/ai-regulatory-updates/eu-enforcement)) ([AI Regulation News October 2026, Cubbbix](https://cubbbix.com/blog/ai-regulation-october-2026-global-update))

5. **AI incident-report standards remain informal.** The International AI Safety Report
   2026 and multiple industry-watch posts describe a landscape with no common incident
   report schema with causal-trace and severity fields. Rule R6 (level-tagged incident
   reports with causal traces) of the threads names exactly this gap. **Candidate for a
   future week** (see "Rejected" below). ([International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)) ([AI Safety October 2026 Update](https://aitechmodel.com/ai-safety-october-2026-update-what-changed-why-it-matters-and-what-to-watch-next/))

## What we chose to build this week

Two artifacts, both named in the lab's candidate pool (`CHARTER.md`), both grounded in the
signal above.

### `projects/2026-10-26_probeset/` (harness)

Signal 1 above. A versioned, scorable set of six prompt probes with a deterministic grader
for the common failure wordings - a drop-in regression suite for the rules a system prompt
most often fails to carry. Six probes, twelve HIGH / MEDIUM fail-patterns, five pass-pattern
INFO signals, standard library only, 63 tests. The grader checks wording, not reasoning;
the scope is in the README and in every result payload.

### `projects/2026-10-26_gatecheck/` (linter)

Signal 2 above. A linter for commitment records against the gate-versus-target discipline
(Rule A11 of the threads). Twelve rules, severities HIGH / MEDIUM / INFO, every rule
documented. A schema (`commitment.schema.json`) and a tiny standard-library-only validator;
a scanner that catches the shapes the industry-watch finding flagged (competitor-contingent
without a named signal, escape clauses broader than the gate's trigger, self-attested
enforcement, prose-only triggers, stale reviews). 64 tests.

Both projects:
- clean-room (implemented from the threads data, not from any third-party tool);
- Apache-2.0 for code, CC-BY-SA-4.0 for adapted material;
- author line "a human-machine hybrid intelligence", no vendor or operator names;
- ASCII punctuation throughout, legacy-console-safe reports, no dependencies.

## Rejected this week

- **`capcard`** (R1 / A13: configuration-keyed capability cards + preference-provenance
  cards). The signal is strong (item 3 above), but the schema and the audit heuristics are
  more design than can be delivered to a world-class bar inside one build. Candidate for a
  future week.
- **`incidenttrace`** (R6 / S6: level-tagged incident reports with causal traces). Strong
  signal (item 5 above), but the public-incident corpus for a worked example is still
  informal; a schema-plus-validator that is useful to an auditor needs at least one solid
  dataset of exemplars to lint, not just prose rules. Candidate for a future week.
- **`registercal`** (M1: calibration of user trust by claim standing). The lesson is
  foundational to the threads, but the "calibration" measurement needs user-study design
  and real evaluation participants to produce an honest metric; not a one-week build.
